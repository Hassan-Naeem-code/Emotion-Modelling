\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:experiments}

\paragraph{Data.} We use a public redistribution of the AffectNet VA
annotations~\cite{mollahosseini2017affectnet} with its own partition:
$58{,}803$ training, $3{,}500$ validation, and $5{,}000$ test images, each with
continuous VA in $[-1,1]^2$ and a categorical expression label. The partition
differs from the official one in two ways that matter here. First, the training
and validation splits are class-balanced (validation has $500$ images of each of
seven expressions), while the test split follows the natural, heavily imbalanced
distribution (from $2{,}354$ \emph{happy} down to $59$ \emph{disgust}). Second,
\emph{contempt} appears only in the test split ($n=70$). We exploit both: the
first is a label shift between calibration and test data, and the second gives
an unseen expression. All seen-data metrics exclude contempt, which is reported
separately.

\paragraph{Protocol.} We hold out a fixed $5\%$ of the training split
($2{,}940$ images, identical for every run) for checkpoint selection. The
validation split is used \emph{only} for conformal calibration and is never seen
during training or selection, so that calibration and test data are
exchangeable up to the label shift above. (Using one split for both purposes, as
is common, weakens the conformal guarantee.) We set $\alpha=0.1$ throughout.

\paragraph{Training.} All heads share the recipe: AdamW (learning rate
$3\times10^{-4}$, weight decay $10^{-4}$), batch size $64$, gradient clipping at
$1.0$, $6$ epochs with $5\%$ linear warm-up and cosine decay, at
$224\times224$. Augmentation is label-preserving: random resized crops (scale
$0.8$--$1$), horizontal flips, rotations up to $10^\circ$, and mild colour jitter.
It contains no blur, noise, or compression, so the corruptions used at test time
remain unseen. The checkpoint with the lowest held-out training loss is kept (NLL
for the probabilistic heads and MSE for the deterministic one, both proper
scores). Each head is trained with five seeds ($1$--$5$). Training one model
takes about $70$ minutes on an Apple M3 Pro.

\paragraph{Second backbone.} To check that the findings are not specific to
EfficientNet-B0 ($5$M parameters), we repeat the deterministic, Gaussian,
evidential, and MC-dropout runs with ConvNeXt-Tiny~\cite{liu2022convnet}
($28$M parameters, ImageNet-22k pretraining) under the same recipe, protocol,
and analysis, changing only the peak learning rate to $10^{-4}$, as is standard
for ConvNeXt. We train three seeds ($1$--$3$) per head; one model takes about
$12$ minutes on an NVIDIA A100.

\paragraph{Holding out each expression.} Contempt is a single unseen
expression with $70$ test images. To test whether its failure is general, we
repeat the evidential run seven times, each time removing one seen expression
$k$ from the training split (including the selection slice) and from the
calibration split, and keeping the test split unchanged. Contempt remains unseen
in every run and is excluded from all groups. For each run we report joint
coverage on the test faces of expression $k$; as a reference, coverage on the
same faces in the main runs, where $k$ was seen; and coverage on the remaining
seen expressions. MC-dropout is evaluated from the same weights. Because the
training split caps most classes at $10{,}000$ images, removing one expression
leaves $46{,}363$--$52{,}250$ training images. We train three seeds ($1$--$3$) per held-out
expression; one model takes about $35$ minutes on an
NVIDIA T4.

\paragraph{Second dataset.} AFEW-VA~\cite{kossaifi2017afew,dhall2012afew}
contains $600$ video clips from films with per-frame VA annotations
($30{,}051$ frames), given as integers in $[-10,10]$, which we divide by $10$. It
differs from AffectNet in source (film rather than web images), modality (video
frames), and annotators, and it has no expression labels. We train, calibrate,
and test on AFEW-VA alone, with the same recipe and protocol as above. Because
the frames of one clip are near-duplicates, we split by clip, never by frame:
$60\%$ of clips for training ($10\%$ of these held out for checkpoint selection),
$20\%$ for calibration, and $20\%$ for testing ($16{,}294$ / $1{,}925$ /
$6{,}061$ / $5{,}771$ frames). The split is fixed across seeds. Since frames
within a clip are correlated, we report coverage both per frame and averaged per
clip, so that each of the $120$ test clips counts once. We train the
deterministic, Gaussian, and evidential heads with three seeds each (MC-dropout
and three-member ensembles are formed from them as before); one model takes
about $45$ minutes on an NVIDIA T4.

\paragraph{Third dataset: text.} To test whether the findings are specific to
faces, we repeat them on a different modality. EmoBank~\cite{buechel2017emobank}
contains $10{,}062$ English sentences rated for valence, arousal and dominance
on a $1$--$5$ scale; we use valence and arousal, mapped to $[-1,1]$. The
sentences come from seven kinds of text (blogs, essays, fiction, letters,
newspapers, news headlines from SemEval, travel guides), which play the role
the expressions play on AffectNet: they define the groups for the audit, and
the held-out-group experiment removes one of them at a time from training and
calibration. We use the official split ($8{,}062$ / $1{,}000$ / $1{,}000$
sentences): training ($10\%$ held out for checkpoint selection), the
development set for conformal calibration, and the test set. The text encoder
(RoBERTa-base~\cite{liu2019roberta}) is frozen; each sentence is represented by
the mean of its last hidden layer, and the same deterministic, Gaussian and
evidential heads are trained on top of it, with input dropout $0.2$ for
MC-dropout, five seeds for the main runs and three for each held-out genre.
Embedding and all heads take a few minutes on a laptop. The calibration and test sets have
only $1{,}000$ sentences each, so a single split carries about one point of
sampling error in coverage; we therefore also report coverage averaged over
$200$ random re-splits of the pooled development and test sentences.

\paragraph{Distribution shift.} As a covariate shift with fixed labels, we apply
Gaussian blur, Gaussian noise, brightness, contrast, and JPEG compression to the
test images at severity $3$, cycling through the five types by image index in the
spirit of ImageNet-C~\cite{hendrycks2019benchmarking}. The conformal quantile is
calibrated on clean validation data and applied unchanged to corrupted inputs.

\paragraph{Metrics.} \emph{Accuracy:} CCC per axis and RMSE.
\emph{Probabilistic quality:} NLL and CRPS (proper scoring
rules~\cite{gneiting2007strictly}; CRPS uses the Gaussian with the predicted mean
and variance), and quantile calibration error
(ECE)~\cite{kuleshov2018calibrated}. \emph{Selective prediction:} area under the
risk--coverage curve (AURC). \emph{Coverage:} joint coverage of the true VA point
and mean region area, overall and per expression. \emph{Detection:} AUROC of the
uncertainty for clean versus corrupted inputs, and for seen versus contempt.
Single models are reported as mean $\pm$ standard deviation over seeds (five
on AffectNet, three on AFEW-VA); ensembles combine those seeds and are reported
once.

\paragraph{Reproducibility.} Each model's per-sample predictions are saved once
(\texttt{eval/dump\_preds.py}), and every number in this paper is computed from
them by one script (\texttt{eval/analyze.py}). The per-sample predictions of all
$32$ model files ($20$ EfficientNet, $12$ ConvNeXt) are released with the code (model outputs and image ids only;
the labels are re-attached from the user's copy of the dataset, which we do not
redistribute), so every table and figure can be recomputed without a GPU
(\texttt{make v2-tables}), and
\texttt{make v2-verify} checks the tables in this paper against them. The
held-out-expression and AFEW-VA tables are produced by the same scripts
(\texttt{eval/loeo.py}, \texttt{eval/analyze.py}) from their own prediction
files, and the copula and recalibration comparisons by
\texttt{eval/joint\_regions.py} and \texttt{eval/recalibrate.py}, also without
a GPU. The EmoBank experiment, from download to table, is
\texttt{eval/emobank.py}. \codestatement
