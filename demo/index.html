The problem

A smile is not proof of happiness.

People smile when they're nervous, polite or tired. A model trained on photos can only learn what faces look like. So this demo never claims to read your feelings. It reports what it can actually measure, and how much to trust it.

  • ✕“You are happy.”
  • ✓“Your face displays a pleasant, mildly activated expression. Here's the 90% range.”
  • ✓“Not sure.” when light, pose or the expression itself is outside what the model knows

What it measures

Three signals. Each with an honest error bar.

Facial expression

Valence (pleasant ↔ unpleasant) and arousal (calm ↔ activated), with a joint 90% region from conformal prediction. Trained on AffectNet.

EfficientNet · evidential head

Pulse from video (experimental)

Each heartbeat changes your skin colour very slightly. The POS rPPG algorithm finds it in your forehead and cheeks, and stays blank when the signal is poor. Experimental: not yet checked against a heart-rate monitor.

rPPG · quality-gated · experimental

Blinks & gaze

Blink rate and the share of time you look at the screen. Behavioural cues of attention and fatigue, never labelled as emotions.
